A Platform for Honest Backtesting, Execution Modeling, and the Alpha Lifecycle

An end-to-end apparatus for evaluating trading strategies under realistic frictions — and a discipline for reporting what does not work.

Author self-directed quant research project Stack Python · NumPy/SciPy/pandas · 276 unit tests Data 20 yr, 2006–2026 · US equities & ETFs
Abstract

We present a self-directed research platform for systematic trading strategies that privileges methodological honesty over headline performance. Candidate strategies are evaluated under a walk-forward protocol behind an overfitting-aware validation gauntlet — a deflated Sharpe ratio, the probability of backtest overfitting, Newey–West standard errors, and a minimum-detectable-Sharpe power analysis — and priced through an execution model (spread, square-root market impact, borrow, and capacity) calibrated against live paper fills. Across eleven candidate strategies spanning cross-sectional factors, statistical arbitrage, and structural order-flow, none establishes a statistically significant edge on liquid, survivorship-constrained data; each null is reported and its cause diagnosed. The platform's contribution is the apparatus itself, together with an implementation framework — the Grinold–Kahn transfer coefficient — for maximizing the fraction of a signal that survives to realized P&L. All headline results are checked on a pre-registered twenty-year out-of-sample hold-out.

§1Research protocol

Every strategy is expressed against one interface and evaluated by an identical engine, so results are comparable and no study can quietly relax the rules. The protocol fixes the assumptions that most often manufacture false alpha:

  1. Walk-forward, out-of-sample by construction. Parameters are fit on a trailing window and traded on the next block; signals at time t use only data through t−1, with a one-period execution lag. The no-look-ahead property is asserted by a unit test, not assumed.
  2. Excess of the risk-free rate. Sharpe ratios are computed on returns net of a T-bill, because a "Sharpe" is compensation for risk above cash — over 2006–2026 the short rate moved 0%→5%, and ignoring it overstates every ratio.
  3. Point-in-time data & survivorship control. A data-quality audit gates on clean inputs; a point-in-time universe masks names to their as-of membership and realizes delisting losses. (Free data remains survivorship-constrained — see §7.)
  4. Pre-registration. For the flagship study the out-of-sample window and the hypothesis are recorded before the test is run; the result is reported once, whether or not it agrees with the guess (§6).

§2The validation gauntlet

Backtesting N configurations on one history is a multiple-comparisons problem: the best of many random strategies looks good by construction. Each candidate therefore passes a battery of overfitting-aware statistics before any claim is entertained.

The power analysis is decisive. The smallest Sharpe distinguishable from zero at a fixed confidence scales inversely with the square root of the sample length,

SRmin  ∝  T−1/2(1)

so extending the record from six to twenty years lowers the detectable threshold from ≈ 1.3 to ≈ 0.64. Much of what a short sample calls a "null" is not evidence of no edge — it is an underpowered test, a distinction §6 makes concrete.

§3Execution & cost model

A frictionless backtest answers the wrong question. The realized cost of a trade decomposes into the half-spread paid to cross, temporary market impact, and — for short or levered books — a financing carry. Impact follows the empirical square-root law,

c  =  s/2  +  κ σ √Q/ADV  +  b · (short notional)(2)

with impact coefficient κ in the empirical equity range [0.1, 0.5] (Almgren et al.). A participation cap converts an over-large order into a partial fill, which makes the backtest capacity-aware: sweeping assets under management traces the AUM at which a strategy's own trading erodes its edge. The gap between the frictionless and realistic runs is Perold's implementation shortfall, itself decomposed into execution and opportunity cost.

§4Risk framework

Risk is modeled structurally rather than as a single volatility number. The asset covariance is built from a factor risk model,

Σ  =  B F B⊤  +  D(3)

with factor loadings B, factor covariance F, and specific variance D; the same Σ drives a constrained mean-variance optimizer (factor- and dollar-neutral, with box and turnover caps). Tail risk is measured with a Cornish–Fisher expansion that corrects the Gaussian quantile for the skew S and excess kurtosis K of realized returns,

zcf = z + (z²−1)S/6 + (z³−3z)K/24 − (2z³−5z)S²/36(4)

so reported VaR and expected shortfall reflect fat tails rather than a normal approximation. The layer also produces marginal risk contributions (where the risk actually sits), historical stress replay of the current book, and mandate limit checks.

§5Findings

Eleven strategies were taken through the full apparatus. Consistent with an efficient-market prior on liquid, survivorship-constrained data, none establishes a statistically significant edge. The value is in the diagnosis: every result is reported honestly, and every failure is attributed to a specific cause.

§6A worked example: the transfer coefficient

The realistic path to added value for a researcher without latency infrastructure is not signal discovery but implementation. In the Grinold–Kahn decomposition of the information ratio,

IR  =  IC  ·  √breadth  ·  TC(5)

the transfer coefficient TC is the fraction of a signal's theoretical performance that survives risk limits, costs, turnover, and unintended factor bets. Discovery moves IC; implementation moves TC. Applying the deployment stack to a standard momentum signal — the signal held fixed — neutralizes hidden bets and controls the tail:

The corollary is the discipline the platform most cares about: an honest transfer analysis also refuses to size a signal that only looks like alpha — catching market exposure in an alpha costume before it reaches the book.

§7Limitations & disclosures

The results are bounded by the data available to a self-funded researcher, and are reported with those bounds explicit rather than obscured.

Survivorship bias
The free universe contains only surviving names, which distorts factor tests (§5); the point-in-time machinery is built for a survivorship-free feed.
Paper-fill optimism
Live paper fills carry no market impact or queue position, so the measured cost (§3) is a lower bound on true execution cost.
Single macro cluster
Even 20 years is ≈ 1–2 independent macro cycles; statistical claims remain modest, and are stated as such.
Calibrated, not measured
Impact and borrow coefficients are sourced to the literature and stress-tested for sensitivity; the qualitative conclusions hold across the plausible band.
Contribution

The deliverable is not a strategy but a process: generate a hypothesis, triage it cheaply, backtest it under real frictions, validate it against a live broker, and — the part rarely demonstrated — establish what is not an edge before capital is committed to it.
